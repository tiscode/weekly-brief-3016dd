# Audit, 22 August 2026

Two halves: whether the numbers are right, and what Parallax offers that we are not using.
Fifty-one automated cross-checks now pass; six defects were found and fixed, two of them
material to the analysis sections.

## Defects found

**1. The factor covariance was calibrated to a decade while everything else used a year.**
The most consequential. Factor volatilities came from 553 weekly observations, so the market
factor carried its ten-year volatility of 16.3% while the last year realised 12.6%. Every line
inherited the inflation, ratios of 1.0 to 1.3, and the portfolio read 18.20% volatility against
a sample-matrix 13.24%. Because volatility is the denominator of a Sharpe ratio, every
risk-adjusted figure was understated:

| | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Portfolio volatility | 18.20% | **13.10%** |
| Tracking error | 8.45% | 9.12% |
| Sharpe, the book as held | 0.47 | **0.65** |
| Sharpe, MSCI ACWI | 0.31 | **0.40** |
| Sharpe, optimal risky portfolio | 1.23 | **1.39** |

Tracking error barely moved, because it is driven by the active weights rather than the level of
market volatility, which is why the error survived earlier checks. The factor matrix is now
estimated on the same 51 weeks as the loadings, the betas, the sample matrix and the realised
series, and the ten-year version is reported alongside as the through-the-cycle alternative.

**2. Suncor was quoted on the wrong listing.** The book showed 94.25 Canadian dollars, the
Toronto price, against a position the account holds and marks in New York at 68.42 US dollars.
The upside was right because it is a ratio, but the price beside it was not the one the position
is marked at. Restated on the account's own basis: 68.42, target 75.95, upside +11.01%.

**3. A percentage squared.** Valero's share of active risk printed as 1135.05%. `book.py`
already converted the fraction and `brief.py` multiplied by 100 again. The nineteen shares now
run from -3.53% to +37.03% and sum to 100.0%.

**4. One missing attribute would have blanked the whole page.** The tab script read
`getAttribute("href").slice(1)` on every navigation link; a single link without an href would
have thrown and left nothing rendered. Guarded.

**5. Sections were hidden by default and revealed by script**, so any thrown error blanked the
page. Inverted: visible by default, hidden only once the script has run, so the page degrades to
one long scroll instead of to nothing.

**6. A renderer was written into the stylesheet.** The anchor comment existed in both the CSS and
the script and the first match won, so the positions table was empty until it was moved.

## Validated against Parallax's own portfolio analytics

`analyze_portfolio` had never been run. Doing so provides an independent check on the
hand-built risk model, over the same year at the same weights:

| | Ours | Parallax |
|---|---:|---:|
| Beta to ACWI | 0.76 | 0.75 |
| Tracking error | 9.12% | 8.23% |
| Portfolio volatility | 13.10% | 11.60% |

Beta agrees to 0.01. The remaining gaps are explained: we sample weekly and they sample daily,
and three lines are priced in local currency here against a US dollar base there. Two
independent implementations landing this close is the strongest evidence yet that the model is
sound.

## One vendor tool that should not be trusted

`check_portfolio_redundancy` returns `has_issues: false` and "Portfolio looks well-diversified"
on this book. The book holds two airlines correlating at 0.910, two parcel carriers at 0.711,
two managed-care names at 0.504, and 90% of its weight in one country. All nineteen lines
resolved, so this is not a coverage failure. The correlation matrix in section 5 found what the
tool missed. Use the matrix.

## Capability available and unused

Ranked by what it would add.

**1. `analyze_portfolio` should become the source of record for performance.** It computes
active return, Sharpe, the information ratio, attribution by market, sector and currency,
drawdown, rolling metrics and turnover, which is most of the guide's performance section. One
practical note: even with a field subset the response ran to 450,000 characters, so it has to be
parsed from file rather than read.

**2. `get_financials` and `get_financial_analysis` would close a real gap.** The value sleeve is
selected on a value score and has never been tested against a multiple. The builder comparison
showed its own value book at a median price to earnings of 13.8 and price to book of 0.58. We
cannot currently say what ours costs. A sleeve called value that has never been priced is a
presentation risk.

> **Superseded, 23 August.** The sleeve is now priced, in `vendor-2026-08-23.md`. Two things
> came back differently from what this paragraph expected. Ours costs a median 15.1 times
> earnings against the momentum sleeve's 13.9, so the value sleeve is the more expensive half
> of the book. And the builder comparison quoted here has to be withdrawn: all six names in
> the builder's value book trade outside the United States, and every market-value ratio the
> vendor returns for a non-dollar line is wrong by factors of two to ten, so 13.8 and 0.58
> were never figures to measure against.

**3. `explain_methodology` gives each factor an academic citation and a documentation link.**
Tactical returns Kyle (1985) and Campbell, Grossman and Wang (1993), and names its inputs as
institutional flows, insider activity, technical signals and event catalysts. The integrity
rules require every number to be explainable in the presentation, and this is the citation for
the one score the whole gate rests on.

**4. `get_peer_snapshot` scores a name against its own peers** rather than against the whole
universe. Our gates ask whether a stock is cheap against everything; the peer view asks whether
it is cheap against its own industry, which is the better question for a single-industry call.

**5. `get_technical_analysis` returns support and resistance, RSI and MACD.** Our momentum sell
rule is a hand-built 8% band off the 252-day high. A real support level would be a better
trigger and is already computed.

**6. `list_docs` shows 37 documentation pages**, including methodology on scoring and factors
and an API reference for the portfolio analyser. Worth reading before the write-up rather than
after.

## What I would do next, in order

1. Run `analyze_portfolio` every week and take the performance section from it rather than
   recomputing it. It is free and it is the vendor's own arithmetic.
2. Pull `get_financials` on the seven value names and put a price on the value sleeve.
3. Add a market or currency cap to the momentum screen. The market HHI of 0.814 is the single
   largest structural weakness in the book and two independent methods now agree on it.
4. Quote `explain_methodology` in the deck's methodology slide.
