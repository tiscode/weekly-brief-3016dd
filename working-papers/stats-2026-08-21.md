## Module 6. The frontier and the optimal risky portfolio

Long only, 25% ceiling per line, weights summing to one, on the factor covariance matrix. Expected returns are the analyst view where one exists and the CAPM required return where none does.

| Portfolio | Expected return | Standard deviation | Sharpe |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Optimal risky portfolio | +21.7% | 14.2% | +1.23 |
| Minimum variance | +7.7% | 8.6% | +0.41 |
| The book as held, ex cash | +12.6% | 18.2% | +0.47 |
| Equal weight, 19 lines | +11.9% | 16.2% | +0.48 |
| MSCI ACWI alone | +9.2% | 16.3% | +0.31 |

The optimal risky portfolio holds:

- AER 25.0% (held 5.8%)
- SU 23.5% (held 2.9%)
- UAL 14.5% (held 5.6%)
- DAL 10.0% (held 5.7%)
- UNH 9.7% (held 2.8%)
- AIZ 9.3% (held 6.0%)
- 00857 6.1% (held 2.9%)
- NUE 1.9% (held 5.4%)

It drops entirely: GDX, BBY, RIO, ELV, FDX, THRO, TRV, ACWI, BIIB, VLO, UPS.

That is a corner solution, which is the known failure of mean-variance optimisation on estimated inputs and exactly what Lecture 1's limits section warns about. Two lines sit at or near the 25% ceiling, eleven of nineteen are dropped, and the answer is driven by whichever names carry the largest analyst upside. Treat it as a diagnostic on the view rather than as a trade list.

The reason a frontier can be drawn at all is that the analyst view differs from the CAPM required return. Under CAPM every line has zero alpha by construction, so the tangency portfolio collapses to the benchmark and the exercise says nothing. The frontier is a picture of the view, not of the data.

## What these numbers do not carry

- Correlation levels in this price data run below what real markets show. The series are internally coherent: ACWI against SPY is 0.958, the two airlines 0.913 and the two parcel carriers 0.708, all plausible. But the average across ten US large caps is 0.177 against roughly 0.35 in real data, so read the absolute correlation and the diversification it implies with caution and rely on the comparisons rather than the levels.
- The risk-free rate is a 2-year yield standing in for a bill rate, because no bill or SOFR series exists in the data and BIL's price is flat by construction. A true bill rate sits below 4.18%, so every Sharpe ratio here is a little low.
- 51 weekly observations. A standard deviation on this sample carries a standard error near 10% of its own value, and a correlation near 0.14 in absolute terms.
- Suncor and Rio Tinto are priced in Canadian and Australian dollars, 5.3% of the fund, and their currency component is not in these numbers. PetroChina is in Hong Kong dollars, which are pegged, so that one is immaterial.
- Benchmark weights are taken as zero for every single name except UnitedHealth, because none of the others appears in the ACWI top 100, so each is below 0.15%. Active weight equals portfolio weight to within that.
- The frontier is in sample. It has not been walked forward, and on this data equal weight has already beaten optimisation out of sample in the earlier rotation work.
